Dean Cameron

Around lunch, Johnny gets up from his desk and stares at the wall. He takes three deep breaths. In.

Out.

The tight office expands like a lung. It grows lengthened bookshelves. Then Giavanti Ghordie pops out of the ceiling.

Then Johnny hears the key in the door. 

And then he opens his eyes. 

Dean Cameron was born December 25, 1962 in Morrison, Illinois. Marginal theologians posit a correlation, but it never really caught on. If someone asked Johnny, he’d say all scholars have this blindspot. DC’s the closest thing to Jesus we’ll ever get. 

“What are you doing?”

“Nothing.” 

Sam didn’t look at Johnny. She put away her coat and the keys back in her pocket.

“Did you finish?”

“No. I was taking a break.”

She purses her lips when she’s thinking. 

“Skip Ski School. Go straight to two.”

“Ok.”

“No. Do that on your own time. Keep looking into the 89’ wheat yields and subsequent UNICEF collapse. Dean was very interested in global farming while filming Rockula.”

"Ok."

Her keyboard springs were rusted and made a sound like a dying ferret. Johnny thinks she likes it. He turns away from the wall. 

Until college Johnny thought he was insane. His high school teachers bored him. His classmates too ignorant. 

One night, past Alpha House, Young Co-Eds, Summer School –– Johnny found himself hovering over Giavanti Ghordie’s 2016 keynote: 

The World According to Dean Cameron.

“We have long debated Truth. Desire. Influence. We revel in dialects of intellect. The whole while wallowing in our ignorance. No longer, colleagues. No longer do we suffer. American Character Actor Dean Cameron, holds the answers to all questions.” 

Johnny was accepted to Harvard’s controversial Cameronology department. He founded the Dean Cameron Film Appreciation Club. Held Treasurer for the Dean Cameron Social History Society. In his four year undergraduate career he gained a reputation for disrupting classes by standing on his desk and equating all of western knowledge to Dean Cameron. Classes about Biology. But in his garnered attention on campus, he got exactly what he wanted.

In retrospect there were four things he should’ve noticed about the office. The lack of bookshelves was the first. Ghoride was an avid reader. The next was the size. A small oak desk barely fit between the plastered walls. The last two reasons left his mind when the door opened.

“Oh. It’s you.” She knew who she was. Johnny thought her glasses were too big.  

“Jonathan Tirsch. Johnny. I’m here to meet Ghordie.”

“No. You’re here for me. Samantha Willux. Sam.”

Sam had him skim discs, drives, cassettes, papers, fliers, napkins, archives, and the Library of Congress. With every report he put on her desk he would ask the same question and get the same answer:

“No.” 

It was when he stopped asking that he started staring at the wall during lunch. 

It wasn’t unheard of for scholars to form an unhealthy parasocail relationship with Dean. The psychology department of the Society even provided a name: Marshak Syndrome. Hailing from his character in Ski School; the famous adage being:  “the snow ‘blinds’ our perception of proximity”.

There are 15 departments within the Cameronology Soceity. Archaeology, Philosophy, Ontology, Epistemology, Sociology, Psychology, History, Media Studies, Economics, Theology, Cryptology, Ecology, Ideology, Research and a Scholarly Improv Troupe. 

Sam is the head of the Research. Johnny is her assistant. 

She kept the key to her desk under the ferret keyboard. Johnny knew she had left when he heard the door close. He didn’t know what he was looking for. An address, a room number.

The man himself was elusive. Spearheading such a substantial field of study led to many enemies. His office was never revealed to the public. The answer Johnny received with each petulant inquiry. But she had to know. 

Then the door opened.

"What are you doing?”

“Nothing. I was looking to see if you had print outs of Cameron’s speech for the 2024 Nobel Peace Prize.”

“Hm. No.”

"Oh."

Sam started staying in the office for lunch. 

In 2016, Ghordie was seen as a pseudo scientist. At least to people who were kind. The rest called him insane. But then came the day everyone remembers where they were. Johnny was getting frozen yogurt. 

Dean Cameron Saves The World. 

It was true. All of human knowledge could be analyzed, criticized, and theorized through the lens of Dean Cameron. It wasn’t long until the private equity firms began investing:

Bikinis and Sanctions: Dean Cameron’s Influence on Economic Policy.

Soft Powder: Geopolitical Mediation in Dean Cameron’s Ski School.

Politics are Dead: A Dialogue Between Rockula and Rising Tensions in the Rust Belt.

Miracle Beach: Consumer Fantasies and the Disilluionment of the Proletariat.

Sam made Johnny stop staring at the wall during lunch.

“It’s relaxing.”

“It’s creepy.”

She started bringing him food and he stopped. 

“Why do you like Dean Cameron?” Sam asked him one day. Johnny dropped his fork.

“What?”

Sam didn’t register his confusion. She just looked at him through her big glasses, waiting for an answer.

“It’s obvious to me. I was confused. I was so confused it hurt. But then I find him. And he has answers. And no one knows. People still don’t see it. So I do everything I can to make them.”

Sam nods. Purses her lips.

“Wrong Answer.” 

Johnny got his moment when Sam was called out to speak with Ghordie. He counted five seconds and took off his shoes. If he hugged the walls she couldn’t see him. 

Two lefts. Then a right. Two flights of stairs. An elevator. One floor up. 

He knew which door it was because he saw the end of her shoe in it. Johnny stared at the unopened door, thinking of what to say. He could talk about Cameron’s humanitarian efforts in Hawaii or how his fascination with house plants helped stabilize the timber industry. He decided to just burst in

“Oh. You followed me.” Sam on one of a set of velvet chairs in front a large mahogany desk, with a thick leather chair. No one in it. 

“He was called to Paris. I’m just leaving his next paper here.” 

When Johnny finally regained sensation he saw the hunk of pages:

Rockula and the Surplus of UNICEF Wheat Yield.

“But––”

“It’s Gordie’s” 

“Oh. Ok.” 

Sam let him go home early. Johnny walked in circles around his block. Over and over. He stood in tall grass outside his house and thought about something from highschool.

Silence filled between Johnny’s chews. His tooth hurt. Sam sifts through penscratched papers while she picks at her caprese salad. Ghordie was nominated for Contribution of The Year. So silence was expected.

“I had your answer.”

“What?”

“I used to have your answer.”

Sam shook the loose papers. It sounded like a wheat field.

“But now I know it’s wrong.” 

Johnny stood up. He grabbed his computer. He flipped the screen. Dean Cameron wears a viking helmet and Santa’s coat. He inhales helium and recites Hamlet. Sam smiled.

“You know they wanted him to recite Bible verses. He refused.”

“It’s what led him to Gutenheim.”

Sam struck her fork in a mozzarella ball. 

The seats were made of felt. Johnny couldn’t sit still. The announcer was monotonous. So were his clothes. Johnny thought he had a lisp and was not yet sure. But that was on the back burner. All of Johnny’s attention was in his arms and his ears. Waiting. 

“Lastly, the prestige to coincide with the prestigious. Contribution of The Year.” 

Johnny rubbed his palms. The man fiddled with the envelope.

“Surprising no one. Giavanti Ghordie, for his valiant and consistent contribution to Cameronology and it’s articulate illumination of mystery.”

The crowd made its way to its feet. Johnny adjusted his hands to the arm bars to propel him up. 

Sam grabbed him. Lightly, on his forearm, as if to make notice of a bird call, or a light turning on. 

“Whatever you are about to do, however you think this will help, it won’t.” 

“But it’s not his.”

And it’s not mine either. Or yours. Or anyone's. That’s my answer. ”

Johnny sat down. The room standing, Sam and Johnny stay seated. Until the lights turned off.

Rats

The rats kept getting out of their cages and into the raisin bran. We fired John soon after. He was the only one who ate raisin bran. The experiments were trivial. We cut zippers out of jackets and clamped them to the cage, doused them with chemicals, and classified the different ways they could unzip.

We settled on four categories:

1. Haste. Like when you're about to have sex.

2. Suave. Like James Dean

3. Morose. My personal favorite. Like when your jacket is the only thing giving you comfort, forced to be released.

4. Not going for the zipper at all, escaping the cage, and finding the nearest source of raisin bran.

After discovering the latter, the rats had no interest in zipping or unzipping at all. Their entire feeble existence became focused on escape. We tried swapping out the rats, but they seemed to all be in communication.

The best part of my day used to be watching their little paws sullenly grasp a zipper. Now my eyes hurt from staring at them constantly, and pushing them away from the latch. In a board meeting I had suggested that we refocus the study on the different ways they attempted escape. It was shot down immediately. Something about optics and shareholders.

I was a glorified vermin babysitter. When people asked me what I’m doing with my time, I lied. I began to notice changes when it came to zipping up my jackets. I even tried to demonstrate to a group of rats how I liked to zip and unzip. Smoothly, with an edge of mystery, like even I didn’t know what was underneath.

-

The box juts slightly out of my pocket, giving my step a new gait to it. I hope no one will notice. The last board meeting had me walking aimlessly in the street. After some slight petulance and erratic annoyance from the head of the board, I discovered what we were really doing with these rats.

Now you would think as one of the scientists I would not only know, but be deeply situated. This is not the case with firms like ours. You get data and send it off. That didn’t keep me from guessing. I had it whittled down to something with motorskills, to allow paraplegics some grace when it came to their morning routines.

I was wrong. We just made zippers. I told my sister the company and she knew it well, which I took as a bad sign.

My answers must have been timid and rushed, as I had a crowd of my coworkers behind me asking politely to stop. It was too late. The gait got more noticeable. Their questions turned to statements. I locked the door behind me, met with pleas. I took the box out of my pocket and walked to the cages of rats.

It was a symphony of chirps. A metropolis. They each looked at me with what I can only describe as pity. Those beady little wonders. Like olives. Or caviar.

I ripped off the lid and let jagged contents pour. Watched as they all scurried towards it. Their little paws no longer looped in metal, now full of the finest raisin bran I could find.
